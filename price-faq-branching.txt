# ✅ The core idea you should adopt

For a multi-tenant chatbot like yours, the best model is:

> **Hybrid model = FAQ + light qualification + progressive estimation + human fallback**

Not:

* pure FAQ ❌
* rigid decision tree ❌
* pure AI guessing ❌

But a **layered approach** that *feels simple to the customer while still gathering pricing signals in the background.*

---

# 1) How this fits into **ai-chat.support (multi-tenant)**

For your tenant **(mywebsolutions.co.in)** you can structure it like this:

### **Layer 1 — Public FAQ (safe + generic)**

You keep simple FAQs such as:

* “How much does a website cost?”
* “Do you build WordPress sites?”
* “Do you build eCommerce sites?”
* “Do you offer SEO?”
* “Do you work with Laravel?”

These FAQs should **NOT try to calculate price.**
They should only **explain ranges + invite a short chat.**

Example answer your bot can give from FAQ:

> “Website cost typically ranges from ₹10,000 to ₹2,00,000 depending on features.
> I can give you a much better estimate in 3 quick questions — if you like, I can ask them now.”

👉 This avoids frustration and repetition.

---

# 2) Instead of “branching tree”, use **progressive questions**

Your bot should **never dump 7 questions at once.**
Only ask **ONE short question at a time.**

Example flow:

**User:** “How much will a new website cost?”

**Bot (from FAQ + smart add-on):**

> “It depends on what you want your site to do.
> Quick first question:
> 👉 Is your goal mainly to (A) showcase your business or (B) sell products?”

User picks one.

Then the bot asks **only the next relevant question.**

This is **not a rigid tree** — it’s *progressive qualification.*

---

# 3) How to handle tricky cases like **“Get leads” or “Build a web app”**

Here’s how your bot can behave **without confusing the customer.**

---

## **Case A — “Get leads”**

Bot logic can be:

> “Great — most lead-generation sites are usually best built in WordPress.
> A typical budget is ₹15,000–₹40,000.
>
> To narrow this down:
> 👉 Do you mainly need (1) a simple contact form or (2) automated leads with WhatsApp, email, and CRM?”

Now you’re learning scope **without tech jargon.**

---

## **Case B — “Build a web app”**

Bot should **not try to price immediately.**

Instead it should say:

> “Web apps vary a lot in cost — from ₹50,000 to several lakhs.
>
> Let me understand your use case in one line:
> 👉 What problem should this app solve?”

That’s a **qualitative question**, not a tree.

Behind the scenes you tag this lead as **HIGH VALUE**.

---

# 4) How you still “estimate” without frustrating users

Use **3 levels of pricing instead of exact numbers:**

Your bot can say things like:

| Situation                   | What the bot says          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Simple site                 | “Usually around ₹10k–₹25k” |
| Business site with features | “Typically ₹25k–₹70k”      |
| Web app / dashboard         | “Often ₹70k+”              |

This keeps customers comfortable and avoids arguments.

---

# 5) How to blend this with your **existing FAQ system**

For your tenant you can design FAQs like:

### FAQ: “How much does a website cost?”

Answer:

> “Costs usually range from ₹10,000 to ₹2,00,000 depending on requirements.
>
> If you want, I can estimate it in 2–3 quick questions — should I proceed?”

Then if user says **Yes**, the bot switches to conversation mode.

---

# 6) When should the bot *stop* and pass to you?

Your AI should **escalate to you** when any of these happen:

* User says “I want a web app”
* User says “I need a custom system”
* User says “I want Laravel”
* User says “I want Magento”
* User mentions dashboards, roles, reports, or real-time features

At that point the bot can say:

> “This sounds like a custom project.
> I’ll collect a few details and our team will respond with a proposal.
> Can I take your email?”

That turns chat into a **lead capture tool**, not a price calculator nightmare.
